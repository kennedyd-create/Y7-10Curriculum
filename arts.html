Purpose of The Arts

Te toi whakairo, ka ihiihi, ka wehiwehi, ka aweawe te ao katoa.
Artistic excellence makes the world sit up in wonder.

The Arts focuses on human creativity, self-expression and cultural communication, through Dance, Drama, Music and the Visual Arts. It builds students' creativity and confidence to express ideas, emotions and experiences.

Students engage with toi Māori and artistic practices from New Zealand, the Pacific and the wider world, and become informed, creative citizens who contribute to cultural understanding and community connection.

The four strands

Dance
Exploring and creating; observing, responding and performing.
Drama
Making and creating; observing and responding.
Music
Listening and responding; performing; improvising and composing; elements of music; music technology (Years 9–10, optional but highly recommended).
Visual Arts
Making and creating; observing and responding.

Years 7–8: students are taught all four strands. Years 9–10: students must be taught at least one strand each year, and it can be a different strand in Year 9 and Year 10.

The sub-strands are designed to be taught together and woven into a single learning process, not in a fixed order. A context statement above each strand suggests the range of artistic experiences students should meet across the phase.

Knowledge and practices

Knowledge sets out the content to teach and how deep to go at each year level. Practices set out the skills, strategies and applications to teach. Both are teaching requirements, not achievement objectives.

  • They relate but are not an exact match. A practice can connect to more than one knowledge statement, and some knowledge has no matching practice.
  • The teacher decides the direction: start with the knowledge and use the practices to consolidate it, or start with a practice and make the links to the knowledge explicit.
  • Anything marked "e.g." is an illustration. You can substitute examples that better reflect the local environment, community, hapū and iwi connections, student interests or teacher expertise, as long as the intended knowledge and practices are taught.

On this site, a Same for Years 7 and 8 label means the document gives one statement that covers both years of the phase.

In Dance, Drama and the Visual Arts the knowledge of both sub-strands is enacted in the practices. In Music the knowledge of the four sub-strands is drawn together into shared practices within each year, grouped under Listening and responding, Performing, and Improvising and composing.

Years 7–8 and 9–10

Phase 3 · Years 7–8

Students refine their creative practice through exploration, rehearsal, iteration and reflection. They develop performances focusing on character, mood, sequences and thematic intent, compose music using harmony and layering, and create artworks using symbolism and composition.

Teaching includes contemporary works from New Zealand, the Pacific and global contexts, and students create work that represents their own lives and identities.

Phase 4 · Years 9–10

Students create layered, intentional works that respond to cultural, historical and social contexts. They analyse artworks using discipline-specific terminology and make creative decisions with tools, techniques and technologies.

They develop choreography, compose music, devise drama and produce visual artworks that reflect complex ideas and stylistic influences.
