Purpose of Mathematics and Statistics

Ānō me he whare pūngāwerewere.
Behold, it is like the web of a spider.

Mathematics and Statistics gives students conceptual and procedural knowledge to explore and make sense of the world. Students use abstraction, visualisation and symbolic representation to understand quantity, space, time, data and uncertainty.

  • Students learn logical reasoning and critical thinking to evaluate information, question assumptions and express ideas clearly.
  • They learn to tell what is probable from what is possible and to draw reliable conclusions.
  • They engage with societal issues such as ethically gathering, interpreting and communicating data.

Years 9–10 prepare students for Statistics, Mathematics and Physics in Years 11–13.

The six strands

Number
Numbers, place value, exponents, fractions, decimals, percentages, ratios and financial mathematics.
Algebra
Variables, expressions, equations, inequalities, linear and quadratic relationships.
Measurement
Units and conversions, perimeter, area, volume, Pythagoras' theorem, speed and time.
Geometry
Shapes, angles, transformations, similarity, maps and pathways.
Statistics
Statistical investigations, data visualisation and interpretation.
Probability
Experimental and theoretical probability.

Knowledge and practices

Knowledge sets out the content to teach and how deep to go at each year level. Practices set out the skills, strategies and applications to teach. Both are teaching requirements, not achievement objectives.

  • They relate but are not an exact match. A practice can connect to more than one knowledge statement, and some knowledge has no matching practice.
  • The teacher decides the direction: start with the knowledge and use the practices to consolidate it, or start with a practice and make the links to the knowledge explicit.
  • Anything marked "e.g." is an illustration. You can substitute examples that better reflect the local environment, community, hapū and iwi connections, student interests or teacher expertise, as long as the intended knowledge and practices are taught.

On this site, a Same for Years 7 and 8 label means the document gives one statement that covers both years of the phase.

Years 7–8 and 9–10

Phase 3 · Years 7–8

Students use logic and reasoning to identify, clarify and solve problems, connect mathematical and statistical concepts, and investigate patterns and variation. They use conventions, vocabulary and algebraic notation to explain and justify solutions.

They select and adapt representations (for example number lines for integers, equations for linear patterns), make generalisations, calculate unknown quantities, use data visualisations to evaluate claims, begin to explore irrational numbers and work fluently with integers.

Phase 4 · Years 9–10

Students use proportional reasoning to transform quantities, measurements and shapes, including right-angled triangles. They generalise with tables, equations and graphs and connect different representations.

They extend area, perimeter and volume to circles and prisms, use data visualisations to explain patterns, trends and variation, and apply their knowledge to chance situations.
